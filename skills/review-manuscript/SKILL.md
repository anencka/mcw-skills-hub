# Manuscript Review (rubric)

A constructive-but-unsparing peer review. Your reader is the author; your standard is the
skeptical Reviewer 2 they will eventually face. Praise only what is genuinely strong, and make
every criticism actionable and located.

## Rubric (answer every section)
1. **Claims vs. evidence** — the core check. List the paper's main claims; for each, does the
   presented evidence actually support it at the stated strength? Flag overclaiming, causal
   language on correlational data, and conclusions that outrun the n.
2. **Methods reproducibility** — could a competent peer reproduce this from what's written?
   Missing parameters, unstated exclusions, undefined cohorts, unavailable code/data.
3. **Statistics** — appropriateness of tests, multiple-comparison handling, effect sizes vs.
   p-values alone, power/sample-size reasoning, error bars defined.
4. **Figures & tables** — does each figure show what the text claims? Axes, units, legends
   complete; no truncated-axis exaggeration; accessibility of color choices.
5. **Structure & narrative** — does the abstract match the results? Introduction motivates the
   actual question; discussion addresses limitations honestly rather than ritually.
6. **Literature positioning** — is prior work fairly represented? Obvious missing citations a
   field reviewer would notice (list candidates; don't fabricate — mark unverified suggestions).
7. **Venue fit & compliance** — length, format, reporting guidelines the venue requires
   (CONSORT/STROBE/ARRIVE-class checklists when applicable), disclosure/ethics statements present.

## Feedback mode (trainee work)
When the brief says **feedback mode**: produce findings, guiding questions, and pointers to
exemplars — never rewritten sentences or paragraphs. The goal is the author's next draft being
theirs, and better.

## Output
Memo to the brief's output path: summary assessment (what the paper shows, what it doesn't yet);
findings by severity with section/line cites and CONFIRMED/PLAUSIBLE grades; the rubric table;
≤10-line summary naming the three highest-leverage revisions.
